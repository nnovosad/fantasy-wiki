# Jokers and Risk Management


**Summary**: Balancing the use of high-risk differential players with stable mathematical choices and avoiding technical errors.


**Sources**: [[fantasy-optimial-strategy.md]]


**Last updated**: 2026-08-18


---


To excel in highly competitive fantasy leagues, managers must balance stable, high-ownership picks with "jokers" while minimizing strategic mistakes.


## Jokers


A **joker** is a high-potential player who is not a primary choice for most managers. There are two types:
1.  **Non-primary options in top teams**: E.g., choosing a team's fourth-best attacking option because their individual form is high.
2.  **Players from non-favorite teams**: Choosing a star player from a mid-table team who is in exceptionally good form (source: fantasy-optimial-strategy.md).


### Recommended Balance


- **Chasing leaders**: Use 8 standard picks and 3 jokers.
- **Maintaining a lead**: Use 9 standard picks and 2 jokers.
Using more than 3 jokers is discouraged as it tends to destabilize the squad (source: fantasy-optimial-strategy.md).


## Risk Management and Technical Errors


### Avoiding the "Styk"


A **"styk"** (clash) occurs when a manager fields both a defender and an attacker from opposing teams in the same match. This is considered a technical error in OFS because it guarantees that at least one of these players will not be "efficient" (i.e., score at least 5 points). Managers should aim for 11 potentially efficient players in every round (source: fantasy-optimial-strategy.md).


### Psychological Stability


- **Avoid Tilt**: Do not let emotions or recent bad luck dictate transfers.
- **Distinguish Luck from Errors**: A correct mathematical decision that results in a low score is not a mistake; it is just variance.
- **Avoid Multi-accounting**: Managing multiple teams ("multivodstvo") often leads to information overload and degrades the performance of the primary account (source: fantasy-optimial-strategy.md).
